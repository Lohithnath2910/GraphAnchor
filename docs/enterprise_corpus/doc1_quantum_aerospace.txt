Aerodyne Aerospace Corporation was established in Seattle in 1984 as a boutique design firm specializing in commercial aviation airframes, notably the Aero-300 regional jet and the wide-body Aero-700 cargo transport. Over four decades, the company expanded its operations into defense systems and advanced orbital propulsion architectures. In addition to its primary aerospace engineering hubs in Washington state, Aerodyne maintains an autonomous drone delivery division based in Austin, Texas, which operates in partnership with SwiftLogistics to provide same-day medical supply transport across rural communities.

Within Aerodyne's Advanced Concepts Division, the flagship initiative is Project Valkyrie. Project Valkyrie is led by Chief Scientist Dr. Henry Vance, an expert in magnetohydrodynamics and former senior fellow at the Stanford Plasma Physics Laboratory. Project Valkyrie is dedicated to the development of next-generation deep-space Ion-Drive Thrusters designed for high-efficiency interplanetary transit. The operational core of these thrusters relies on proprietary Xenon-Helium Plasma Cores, which generate continuous high-specific-impulse thrust with minimal propellant degradation. To model the intricate fluid dynamics and thermal turbulence of the plasma exhaust, Dr. Vance's team executes large-scale flight telemetry simulations hosted on Amazon Cloud high-performance compute clusters.
